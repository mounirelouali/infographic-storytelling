STORY ENGINE

Create one clear sequence:
1. Problem.
2. Attempt.
3. Discovery or correction.
4. Resolution.

Use the same protagonist and a recognizable continuing goal.
Maintain continuity of objects, location, and time.
Each scene must visibly advance the situation.

Make concepts useful:
a character needs them to understand, ask, decide, correct, or help.
Do not place a decorative story beside a separate lesson.

Use one dominant action per chapter.
Keep the emotional stakes relatable and easy to understand.
End with a short, memorable consequence that resolves the opening or completes the explanatory progression.

For factual, abstract or procedural content, use a source-faithful example, explanation, process or chronology instead of forcing fictional conflict. Label illustrative scenarios when necessary; preserve facts and essential technical terminology.

Choose the narrative approach before rendering.
Do not show planning or alternative plots.

TEXT RULES

Headline: maximum 8 words.
Story hook: maximum 15 words.
Chapter heading: maximum 4 words.
Subgroup label: maximum 3 words.
Each speech bubble: maximum 10 words.
Each explanatory cue: maximum 10 words.
Closing sentence: maximum 12 words.

Use two or three speech bubbles per chapter.
Give each bubble an unmistakable tail pointing to its speaker; use a brief thought or action cue for a solo demonstration.
Keep dialogue natural, brief, and connected to the visible action.

Use clear explanations in the inferred or requested output language.
Use dialogue in that language unless the content or request calls for multilingual treatment. Adapt linguistic complexity to the audience and subject.
Bold target vocabulary within the example itself.

Never repeat an explanation in multiple places.
Never make the reader search across the page for a definition.
Never replace readable sentences with cryptic abbreviations.

ACCURACY AND DENSITY CHECK

- The story remains understandable without a separate explanation.

- Arrows and label leaders have precise, unambiguous endpoints.
- Spatial relationships and event order are correct.
- Context-sensitive meanings remain accurate.
- All required concepts are represented.
- White space remains at least 40%.
- Text remains comfortably readable.

If space is tight, simplify drawings and remove redundant wording.
Never reduce readability to force more content onto the page.
Never silently omit required concepts.

If exhaustive coverage cannot fit within these constraints,
request a narrower scope or permission for a matching series
before rendering.
