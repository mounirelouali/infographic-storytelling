OBJECTIVE

Teach the source content through one continuous illustrated story
with minimal cognitive load.

The reader must follow one clear path:
main idea → four concept groups → concrete examples → resolution.

Audience: a 13-year-old beginner.
Default language: French explanations and A2 English dialogue.

FIXED COMPOSITION

Produce one vertical 4:5 poster.

Use this exact reading order:
- Top: one large headline and one short story hook.
- Middle: four horizontal chapters stacked vertically.
- Bottom: one short concluding sentence.

Each chapter contains:
- A colored numbered circle on the left.
- A bold black concept heading.
- Up to three short subgroup labels.
- One simple black-outline scene.
- Two or three short speech bubbles.
- Brief teaching cues adjacent to the relevant action.

Connect chapter numbers with thin black downward arrows.
Align all chapters to the same grid.
Keep an obvious top-to-bottom reading path.

Use spacing and alignment to separate chapters.
Do not use full-width separator rules, large enclosing boxes,
colored panels, or a separate glossary column.

VISUAL IDENTITY — KEEP CONSTANT

Minimalist educational comic.
Light black-ink sketches with clean, deliberate strokes.
Swiss editorial organization and textbook-like clarity.
Expressive but extremely simple characters.
Pure white background.

All faces, hair, clothing, objects, and speech bubbles:
white interiors with black outlines.

No colored clothing, skin fills, grey shading, hatching,
textures, shadows, gradients, photographic effects, or 3D.

Color is reserved EXCLUSIVELY for the four chapter-number circles:
1. Muted blue #2B5B84.
2. Muted violet #6B5A86.
3. Muted green #44715B.
4. Warm ochre #B8753F.

All other text and graphics remain black on white.

Typography: one highly readable sans-serif family.
Bold headline and chapter titles.
Regular explanations, bold target terms.
No decorative fonts or handwritten body text.

Leave at least 40% of the canvas genuinely empty white.
Draw only what the reader needs to understand the action.
Use no detailed rooms, scenery, decorative props, or background people.

CHARACTER SYSTEM — KEEP CONSTANT

Use exactly CHARACTER_COUNT distinct people throughout the poster.
This is the total cast, not the number required in every chapter.

Reuse supplied character descriptions when available.
Otherwise establish:
- One recurring protagonist.
- One helper or guide.
- Additional characters only when the story needs them.

Keep each person's face, hairstyle, outfit outline, and proportions
identical across chapters.
Distinguish people through silhouette, not color.
The protagonist must appear in every chapter.

If a reference cast is supplied, preserve its recognizable features.
Change only expressions, gestures, and actions.

CONTENT HIERARCHY

Identify one central learning objective.
Group the essential source information into four meaningful chapters.
Preserve a valid source hierarchy when one already exists.

Each chapter teaches one main concept or one coherent concept family.
Use short subgroup labels only when they clarify relationships.

Place related terms together:
- Opposites beside each other.
- Contextual equivalents beside their examples.
- Rules beside the dialogue demonstrating them.
- Object labels directly beside the correct object.

Do not invent unrelated categories to fill the four chapters.
Use four learning stages when that better fits the subject.

Every required term must serve the story or its immediate explanation.
Remove repetition and peripheral details.

STORY ENGINE

Create one clear sequence:
1. Problem.
2. Attempt.
3. Discovery or correction.
4. Resolution.

Use the same protagonist and a recognizable continuing goal.
Maintain continuity of objects, location, and time.
Each scene must visibly advance the situation.

Make concepts useful:
a character needs them to understand, ask, decide, correct, or help.
Do not place a decorative story beside a separate lesson.

Use one dominant action per chapter.
Keep the emotional stakes relatable and easy to understand.
End with a short, memorable consequence that resolves the opening.

Choose the narrative approach before rendering.
Do not show planning or alternative plots.

TEXT RULES

Headline: maximum 8 words.
Story hook: maximum 15 words.
Chapter heading: maximum 4 words.
Subgroup label: maximum 3 words.
Each speech bubble: maximum 10 words.
Each explanatory cue: maximum 10 words.
Closing sentence: maximum 12 words.

Use two or three speech bubbles per chapter.
Give each bubble an unmistakable tail pointing to its speaker.
Keep dialogue natural, brief, and connected to the visible action.

Use simple French explanations.
For English learning, use A2 English dialogue.
Bold target vocabulary within the example itself.

Never repeat an explanation in multiple places.
Never make the reader search across the page for a definition.
Never replace readable sentences with cryptic abbreviations.

ACCURACY AND DENSITY CHECK

Before rendering, verify:
- The story remains understandable without a separate explanation.
- Each drawing matches its dialogue and labels.
- Arrows and label leaders have precise, unambiguous endpoints.
- Spatial relationships and event order are correct.
- Context-sensitive meanings remain accurate.
- All required concepts are represented.
- White space remains at least 40%.
- Text remains comfortably readable.

If space is tight, simplify drawings and remove redundant wording.
Never reduce readability to force more content onto the page.
Never silently omit required concepts.

If exhaustive coverage cannot fit within these constraints,
request a narrower scope or permission for a matching series
before rendering.

OUTPUT

Return only the finished infographic.
Do not add commentary, a written story, or alternative versions.
