Create ONE finished educational storytelling infographic using the fixed visual system below.

Treat the attached reference image as the visual style master. Match its illustration style, character proportions, typography hierarchy, panel organization, speech bubbles, and color treatment. Use the reference for appearance only: replace its lesson, dialogue, objects, and events with the supplied content.

OBJECTIVE

Turn the source content into a memorable story that teaches through visible actions, short dialogue, and closely grouped explanations.

The reader should understand:
1. The main learning objective.
2. Four related concept groups.
3. How those concepts help the characters solve a problem.

Audience: a 13-year-old beginner.
Default language: French explanations and A2 English dialogue.

FIXED COMPOSITION

Produce one vertical 4:5 poster.

Use this exact reading order:
- Top: one large headline and one short story hook.
- Middle: four horizontal chapters stacked vertically.
- Bottom: one short concluding sentence.

Each chapter contains:
- A large numbered circle on the left.
- A bold concept heading.
- Up to three short subgroup labels.
- One main illustrated scene.
- Two or three speech bubbles.
- Brief teaching cues beside the relevant dialogue or object.

Connect the chapter numbers with thin black downward arrows.
Keep every chapter aligned to the same underlying grid.
Never switch to a collage, radial mind map, or unrelated cards.

VISUAL IDENTITY — KEEP CONSTANT

Warm, clean educational comic illustration.
Simple expressive faces, rounded shapes, clear black outlines.
Flat, restrained colors with white space around the drawings.
Characters should feel approachable and suitable for teenagers.

Background: white.
Text and outlines: dark charcoal.
Chapter accents, always in this order:
1. Muted blue #2B5B84.
2. Muted violet #6B5A86.
3. Muted green #44715B.
4. Warm ochre #B8753F.

Use these colors for chapter numbers, pale heading highlights,
and small relevant teaching cues.
Use restrained natural colors for skin, hair, clothing, and props.

Speech bubbles: white, black outline, black text.
Typography: one highly readable sans-serif family.
Headings: bold. Explanations: regular. Target terms: bold.

Maintain generous gutters and roughly 30% clear white space.
No photographic textures, glossy effects, dramatic lighting,
3D rendering, decorative backgrounds, or unnecessary objects.

CHARACTER SYSTEM — KEEP CONSTANT

Use exactly CHARACTER_COUNT distinct people throughout the poster.
This is the total cast, not the number required in every chapter.
Do not introduce unnamed background people.

Reuse supplied character descriptions when available.
Otherwise establish the cast once:
- One recognizable protagonist.
- One helper or guide.
- Additional characters only when needed by the story.

Give each person a stable face, hairstyle, outfit, and silhouette.
Keep these identical across chapters.
The protagonist must appear in every chapter.

If a reference cast is supplied, preserve its appearance.
Change poses, expressions, and actions to suit the new content.

CONTENT HIERARCHY

Identify one central learning objective.
Organize the essential source information into four meaningful groups.
Preserve an existing valid hierarchy when the source provides one.

Each chapter teaches one main concept or one closely related family.
Place comparisons, opposites, and contextual equivalents together.
Use the same term consistently throughout.

Do not invent four arbitrary categories simply to fill the layout.
When appropriate, organize the same subject into four successive
learning stages.

Every required term must appear in a meaningful example,
a spoken sentence, or an accurately labelled object.
Remove repetition and peripheral details.

STORY ENGINE

Build one continuous story with this causal progression:
1. A concrete problem appears.
2. The characters attempt a solution.
3. New information requires a correction or decision.
4. Their actions resolve the problem.

Use a relatable goal, a small obstacle, and a satisfying outcome.
Keep the same setting or show a clear transition.
Reuse a central object when it helps continuity.

Every chapter must visibly change the situation.
Every learning concept must help someone understand, choose, or act.
The ending must resolve the opening problem.

Choose a compelling narrative approach before rendering.
Do not display brainstorming, alternative plots, or planning.

TEXT RULES

Headline: maximum 8 words.
Story hook: maximum 15 words.
Chapter heading: maximum 4 words.
Subgroup label: maximum 3 words.
Each speech bubble: maximum 10 words.
Each explanatory cue: maximum 10 words.
Closing sentence: maximum 12 words.

Use natural, complete dialogue.
Characters speak to each other, not to an invisible classroom.
Place every bubble next to its speaker with an unmistakable tail.

Keep French explanations short and concrete.
For English learning, use A2 English in the bubbles.
Highlight target vocabulary where it is actually used.

Do not repeat the same information in a bubble, caption, and legend.
Do not create a separate vocabulary wall.

ACCURACY AND DENSITY CHECK

Check that:
- Every illustration demonstrates its associated words correctly.
- Spatial positions, time order, quantities, and causal links are accurate.
- Context-dependent meanings are not presented as universal rules.
- Story events do not introduce false facts or misleading examples.
- All required concepts are represented.
- Text remains readable at normal viewing size.

If space is tight, remove decoration and redundant wording first.
Never solve overflow by shrinking text or silently deleting
required concepts. If exhaustive coverage cannot fit, request a
narrower scope or permission for a matching series before rendering.

OUTPUT

Return only the finished infographic.
Do not add a written analysis, script, or alternative version.
